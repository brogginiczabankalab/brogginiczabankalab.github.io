Our mission

From the operating theatre to the bench — and back again.

Our laboratory is dedicated to advancing the understanding and treatment of neurosurgically relevant disease. As part of the Department of Neurosurgery at University Hospital Frankfurt, we work at the point where clinical observation and basic biology meet.

Disease exploration

Investigating the mechanisms behind brain metastasis, glioblastoma and neurovascular disorders — always anchored to a clinical question.

Advanced imaging

Confocal, two-photon and wide-field microscopy — including adaptive optics — to examine the brain at cellular and system scale.

High-dimensional analysis

Single-cell and high-dimensional expression profiling of patient-derived tissue, benchmarked against mouse models.

Translation to therapy

Working with clinical teams so discoveries reach practice — and so clinical observation keeps steering the laboratory.

Research programme

Four questions we keep returning to.

Each of our lines of work sits at the interface between tumour biology and the cerebral vasculature — the tissue that decides whether a therapy ever reaches its target.

Brain metastasis & the pre-metastatic niche

Before a tumour cell ever arrives, the brain changes. We map metastatic and pre-metastatic tissue at single-cell resolution to find biomarkers that predict — and might prevent — brain and bone metastasis.

Featured work

A washing machine for the brain.

Neuron 2024 Goethe University × UC San Diego

Using a combination of microscopic and biophysical methods, we visualised for the first time that the arterioles diving into the cerebral cortex pulsate independently of the heartbeat and of brain activity. These oscillations alter blood flow by 20% — and the effect is stronger at rest than in active brain regions.

The oscillations generate long-wavelength traveling waves that sweep along every arteriole at about two millimetres per second, in many different directions. The bulges they create on the vessel wall may help mix the cerebrospinal fluid bathing the brain, improving the clearance of misfolded proteins and metabolic waste — a mechanism that is disrupted in Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders.

“In further work here in Frankfurt, we will investigate how the traveling waves are altered during strokes, cerebral haemorrhages and neurodegenerative diseases, and what influence they have on the development of these diseases.” Thomas Broggini — first author, in the Goethe University press release, 31 July 2024

The team

Clinicians, biologists and physicists in one room.

Our interdisciplinary group brings together neurosurgical practice, molecular biology and optical physics — because none of these questions belongs to a single discipline.

PD Dr. Thomas Broggini, PhD

Principal Investigator · Head of Laboratory · Working Group Leader

Thomas Broggini leads the Translational Neurosurgery Laboratory at the Department of Neurosurgery, University Hospital Frankfurt. His work combines optical imaging, small-animal microsurgery and advanced sequencing to understand how metastasis forms — in mouse and in patients — and how the cerebral vasculature governs what reaches the brain.

His route into science was not the usual one: he trained first as a chemistry laboratory technician in Switzerland, worked in industry, and only then studied biotechnology — an apprenticeship-first path he still credits for how he approaches an experiment.
