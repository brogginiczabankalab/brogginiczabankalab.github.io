Welcome

Welcome to the lab.

Starting in a new lab means a lot of small questions at once — where to go, how to get here, who to ask. This page collects the answers we give every new student, so you can spend your first weeks on the science.

General

Your first weeks.

Nobody expects you to know how things work on day one. Ask early and ask often — every person in this group was new here once.

Day one

  • Meet your supervisor — agree on your project, your first experiments and how often you meet
  • Access — staff or student ID card, keys and transponder for Haus 89
  • Accounts — university e-mail, IT login and access to the lab's shared drive
  • A tour — labs, imaging rooms, where things live and where waste goes

Safety first

  • Safety briefings — general laboratory safety, fire safety and hazardous substances, before any bench work
  • Biosafety — instruction for our class 1 and class 2 laboratories before you work in them
  • Equipment — get trained by an experienced member before using a microscope or instrument on your own
  • Every year — safety instructions are renewed annually

Lab life

  • Lab meeting — the whole group, regularly; you will present your own data too
  • Lab notebook — document every experiment on the day you do it
  • Data — raw data belongs on the lab's shared storage, not only on your laptop
  • Outside the lab — see our group activities

Mensa

  • On campus — the staff canteen of the Universitätsklinikum, on the same campus as Haus 89
  • First lunch — ask someone from the group to take you along on your first day
  • Main campuses — the Studierendenwerk Frankfurt runs the Mensas at Westend, Bockenheim and Riedberg

Getting here

By tram, by car, by bike.

We are in the Neuroscience Center, Haus 89, on the campus of the Universitätsklinikum in Sachsenhausen. The address, a map and a route planner are in the contact section.

Public transport (RMV)

  • Closest stop — Heinrich-Hoffmann-Straße / Blutspendedienst, trams 12, 15, 19 and 21
  • From Hauptbahnhof — tram 12 or 21
  • From Südbahnhof — tram 15 or 19
  • Tickets — Goethe University students travel on their semester ticket; employees should ask about the job ticket
  • Planning — timetables and live departures on rmv.de or in the RMVgo app

Parking

  • Main entrance — paid multi-storey car park directly behind the barrier
  • Sandhofstraße — paid car park, entered from Deutschordenstraße behind the blood donation service; the closest to Haus 89
  • Weekday mornings — spaces fill up early; the tram is often faster

Directions to the lab

  • Address — Neuroscience Center, Haus 89, Heinrich-Hoffmann-Straße 7, 60528 Frankfurt am Main
  • Map and route — in the contact section on the home page
  • First visit — let us know when you arrive and someone will meet you at the entrance

Responsibility

Ethical animal practice.

Part of our work depends on animal experiments. That is a privilege granted under strict conditions, and we expect everyone in the group to treat it that way — whether or not you work with animals yourself.

The 3Rs

  • Replace — use cells, tissue or computational models wherever they can answer the question
  • Reduce — plan sample sizes properly and get the most information from every animal
  • Refine — minimise pain and distress through anaesthesia, analgesia, handling and housing

Rules and approval

  • Legal basis — the German Animal Welfare Act (TierSchG), the animal experimentation ordinance (TierSchVersV) and EU Directive 2010/63/EU
  • Approval — every procedure needs a licence from the Regierungspräsidium Darmstadt before it starts
  • Only what is approved — no procedure, animal number or endpoint outside the licence

Education on animal welfare

  • Training first — a certified laboratory animal science course (FELASA-accredited) before you handle animals
  • Supervised practice — hands-on training with an experienced member until you are signed off
  • Animal welfare officer — the institutional animal welfare officer advises on any question

Daily practice

  • Score sheets — monitor and record every animal's condition as the licence requires
  • Humane endpoints — know them before the experiment, and act on them without delay
  • Reporting — we report animal studies following the ARRIVE guidelines
  • Speak up — if something looks wrong, say so; raising a concern is always the right call

Questions?

Ask us.
